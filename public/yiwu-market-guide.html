1. What is Yiwu Market?

Yiwu International Trade City (also called Yiwu Market or Futian Market) is the world's largest wholesale market for small consumer goods. Located in Yiwu City, Zhejiang Province, China, it covers over 14 million square meters of trading space with more than 75,000 booths selling over 2.1 million different products.

Unlike Alibaba or other online platforms, Yiwu Market is a physical place where you can touch, feel, and compare products in person. This makes it ideal for buyers who want to verify quality before placing orders, especially for small businesses that need low minimum order quantities.

Why Buyers Come to Yiwu

  • Low MOQ: Many suppliers accept orders as small as 50-100 pieces
  • Competitive pricing: Direct access to factory-level wholesale prices
  • Physical inspection: See and touch products before you buy
  • Wide variety: From toys to jewelry to kitchenware, everything in one city
  • Customization: Many suppliers offer logo printing and custom packaging
Insider Tip: Most suppliers at Yiwu Market do not list their products online. The only way to see their full catalog and latest prices is to visit their booth in person. This is why having a local agent on the ground gives you a significant advantage over buyers who only use Alibaba.

How to Get to Yiwu

Yiwu has its own airport (Yiwu Airport) with flights from major Chinese cities and some international destinations. The high-speed train from Shanghai takes about 1.5 hours, and from Hangzhou about 30 minutes. Most international buyers fly into Shanghai Pudong Airport and take the train or arrange a car to Yiwu.

3. How to Find Reliable Suppliers

Finding the right supplier is the most critical step in the sourcing process. A good supplier delivers quality products on time. A bad one can cost you money, customers, and your business reputation.

Step 1: Visit Multiple Booths

Never settle for the first supplier you find. Visit at least 5-10 booths selling similar products. Compare prices, quality, minimum order quantities, and the supplier's willingness to work with you. Take photos of product samples, price lists, and business cards.

Step 2: Check the Supplier's Credentials

  • Business license: Ask to see their business registration certificate
  • Years in business: Suppliers with 5+ years are generally more reliable
  • Booth ownership: Booth owners (not renters) tend to be more established
  • Export experience: Ask if they have experience shipping to your country
  • Product specialization: Suppliers focused on one product type usually offer better quality and prices

Step 3: Request Samples

Always request samples before placing a large order. Most suppliers at Yiwu Market will provide samples for free or at a small cost. Pay attention to:

  • Material quality and thickness
  • Workmanship and finishing
  • Color accuracy
  • Packaging condition
  • Functionality (if applicable)

Step 4: Verify the Factory

If you're placing a large order, visit the factory if possible. If you can't visit yourself, ask someone local to go on your behalf. Key things to check:

  • Production capacity and current workload
  • Quality control processes
  • Working conditions
  • Whether they are the actual manufacturer or a trading company
Red Flag: If a supplier refuses to provide samples, won't give you their business license, or pressures you to pay in full upfront without a proper contract, walk away. These are common warning signs of unreliable suppliers.

4. Understanding MOQ & Pricing

MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity) and pricing are where many first-time buyers get confused. Understanding how Chinese suppliers think about pricing will help you negotiate better deals.

How MOQ Works at Yiwu

MOQ at Yiwu Market is generally much more flexible than on Alibaba. Here's what you can expect:

Product TypeTypical MOQNotes
Standard off-the-shelf items50-100 piecesReady-made products with no customization
Custom color or minor changes200-500 piecesSimple modifications to existing designs
Custom packaging500-1,000 piecesPrivate label boxes, inserts
Logo printing300-1,000 piecesDepends on printing method
Fully custom product1,000-5,000 piecesNew mold or design required

Negotiation Tips

  • Buy in volume: The easiest way to get a lower price is to increase your quantity
  • Bundle products: If a supplier makes multiple products you need, order them together for better pricing
  • Pay deposits promptly: Suppliers prefer buyers who pay quickly and are more likely to offer discounts
  • Build a relationship: Repeat customers almost always get better prices than one-time buyers
  • Don't negotiate too hard: Extremely low prices often mean corners are cut on quality

Understanding the Price Structure

When a supplier quotes you a price, understand what's included:

  • EXW (Ex Works): You pay for everything after the goods leave the factory
  • FOB (Free on Board): Supplier delivers goods to the port and handles export customs
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): Supplier arranges shipping to your port

Most Yiwu suppliers quote EXW prices by default. Make sure you clarify the terms and factor in shipping, insurance, and import duties when calculating your total cost.

5. Quality Control Checklist

Quality control is where many sourcing projects fail. The product looks great in the supplier's booth, but what arrives at your warehouse is different. Use this checklist to protect yourself.

Pre-Production Inspection

  • Confirm product specifications in writing (materials, dimensions, colors, weight)
  • Approve production samples before mass production begins
  • Specify acceptable quality standards (AQL level)
  • Define the defect classification system (critical, major, minor)
  • Agree on inspection timing and method

During Production Inspection

  • Visit the factory or have someone visit during production
  • Check raw materials against approved samples
  • Inspect first production units for accuracy
  • Monitor production line conditions and processes
  • Take photos and videos of the production process

Pre-Shipment Inspection (Most Important)

This is your last chance to catch problems before the goods leave China. A proper pre-shipment inspection should include:

  • Verify total quantity matches the purchase order
  • Check product appearance (colors, finish, workmanship)
  • Test functionality if applicable
  • Verify packaging and labeling
  • Check weight and dimensions
  • Inspect for visible defects, scratches, or damage
  • Take detailed photos of random samples from different cartons
  • Open inner packaging to verify individual item quality
Pro Tip: Always inspect products from randomly selected cartons, not just the ones the supplier shows you. Pick cartons from different parts of the shipment to get a representative sample.

6. Shipping Methods Compared

Choosing the right shipping method can make or break your budget and timeline. Here's a clear comparison of your options.

MethodTimeCostBest ForNotes
Sea Freight (FCL) 25-40 days Lowest Large orders (20ft+ container) Most economical for bulk shipments. Full container gives you control over loading.
Sea Freight (LCL) 30-45 days Low Medium orders (1-10 CBM) Your goods share a container. Slightly higher cost per unit and longer transit.
Air Freight 7-12 days Medium Urgent or high-value goods Good balance of speed and cost. Charged by actual or volumetric weight.
Express Courier 3-7 days High Small orders, samples, urgent items DHL, FedEx, UPS. Door-to-door service. Best for orders under 100kg.
Train Freight 18-25 days Medium-Low Europe routes China-Europe rail. Faster than sea, cheaper than air. Growing route network.

Understanding Shipping Costs

Beyond the freight cost itself, budget for these additional charges:

  • Export customs clearance: Usually handled by the supplier or freight forwarder ($50-150)
  • Import customs clearance: In your country, often handled by a customs broker ($100-300)
  • Duties and taxes: Varies by product type and destination country (0-25% of goods value)
  • Port/terminal handling fees: Charged at both origin and destination
  • Delivery from port to your warehouse: $100-500 depending on distance
Cost-Saving Tip: For your first few orders, use a freight forwarder who can handle the entire process door-to-door. Yes, it costs more than arranging each step yourself, but you'll avoid costly mistakes. Once you understand the process, you can start managing individual components to save money.

7. Common Scams & How to Avoid Them

Unfortunately, scams exist in every industry, and international sourcing is no exception. Being aware of the most common scams will help you protect your business.

Scam 1: The Bait and Switch

The supplier shows you high-quality samples, but the mass-produced goods are inferior. This is the most common quality scam.

How to avoid: Always conduct a pre-shipment inspection. Use a third-party inspection company or have your agent check the goods before they ship. Include quality specifications in your contract with penalties for non-compliance.

Scam 2: The Price Increase After Deposit

You agree on a price, pay the deposit, and then the supplier claims the price has gone up due to "raw material cost increases."

How to avoid: Get a written quotation with validity period (usually 30 days). Include a clause in your contract that the price is fixed after deposit payment. Work with suppliers who have been in business for several years.

Scam 3: The Fake Factory

A trading company poses as a manufacturer, takes your order, and outsources production to an unknown factory with poor quality control.

How to avoid: Ask to visit the factory. Check their business license to see if they are a manufacturer or trading company. Ask for production photos during manufacturing. Working with a local agent who can visit the factory for you is the best protection.

Scam 4: The Disappearing Supplier

You pay in full upfront, and the supplier stops responding. Your money and goods are gone.

How to avoid: Never pay 100% upfront. Use the standard payment structure: 30% deposit before production, 70% balance after inspection and before shipment. For larger orders, use a Letter of Credit (L/C) through your bank.

Scam 5: The Hidden Fee Trap

The quoted price seems great, but after you've committed, additional fees start appearing.

How to avoid: Get a detailed quote in writing that specifies exactly what's included. Ask about packaging costs, labeling fees, mold charges, shipping to port, export documentation, and any other potential extra charges before you agree to anything.

Golden Rule: If a deal seems too good to be true, it probably is. Extremely low prices, pressure to pay immediately, suppliers who refuse to provide documentation, and deals that skip normal procedures are all red flags. Take your time, do your due diligence, and never let excitement override caution.

8. Do You Need a Sourcing Agent?

This is one of the most common questions from first-time buyers. The honest answer: it depends on your situation.

When You Can Source Directly

  • You have experience importing from China
  • You can visit Yiwu in person
  • You speak Chinese or have a reliable translator
  • You have time to manage multiple suppliers
  • You understand quality control and shipping logistics

When a Sourcing Agent Helps

  • This is your first time importing from China
  • You can't travel to Yiwu yourself
  • You need someone to visit suppliers and check quality in person
  • You want to focus on selling, not managing suppliers
  • You need help with custom packaging, labeling, or product modifications
  • You want to reduce the risk of scams and quality issues

9. Your First Order: Action Plan

Now that you understand the basics, here's a step-by-step action plan for placing your first order from Yiwu Market.

Week 1: Research & Planning

  • Define your target product(s) with specifications
  • Research your target market and set a target price point
  • Calculate your total budget (product cost + shipping + duties + agent fees)
  • Decide whether to visit Yiwu yourself or work with a sourcing agent
  • If using an agent, prepare a detailed product brief with photos and requirements

Week 2-3: Sourcing & Sampling

  • Visit Yiwu Market or have your agent visit relevant districts
  • Compare 5-10 suppliers for each product
  • Collect samples from your top 3 suppliers
  • Check supplier credentials and export experience
  • Negotiate pricing and MOQ

Week 4: Order Confirmation

  • Approve final samples and confirm product specifications in writing
  • Sign a purchase agreement with quality terms and delivery timeline
  • Pay the deposit (typically 30%)
  • Confirm packaging requirements and labeling

Week 5-8: Production & Inspection

  • Monitor production progress (via agent updates if applicable)
  • Conduct pre-shipment inspection
  • Approve or request corrections based on inspection results
  • Pay the balance after inspection approval
  • Arrange shipping and export documentation

Week 9-12: Delivery

  • Track shipment transit
  • Clear customs in your country
  • Inspect delivered goods against your approved samples
  • Provide feedback to your supplier for future orders

Final Tips for Success

Start small. Your first order should be a test. Order enough to validate quality and sales, but not so much that a problem would hurt your business.

Document everything. Keep records of all communications, agreements, samples, and inspection reports. This protects you if issues arise.

Build relationships. The best prices and service come from suppliers you've worked with multiple times. Treat your first order as the start of a long-term partnership, not a one-time transaction.

Ask for help. There's no shame in not knowing everything. Whether it's a sourcing agent, a freight forwarder, or an experienced fellow importer, getting advice from people who've done this before will save you time, money, and stress.
